How this works

The three structures share one cell grid. Each cell is a 3×3 room interior with a wall line, one storey tall. Cells are grouped into halls (single cells), rooms (open rectangles on one storey) and shafts (climbable columns). A spanning tree over those regions makes every one reachable, and braiding then gives dead ends a second way out. Storeys connect by 3-step stairs (with a hole above) or by shafts.

The Tower has an entrance on the ground floor and its goal on the top floor. The Megastructure is not a building but a volume: habitation clusters hang at many heights in a mostly empty space, joined by bridges and long stairways routed across the void (each stair cell climbs or drops one storey across one cell, so the runs zig-zag up through the gulfs). Monoliths of solid mass stand in the void and prop up some clusters. There is no floor plan; the only promise is that all of it can be walked. The Pyramid narrows from a triangular base to a single top cell. The Sunken column starts at a stair in the surface and descends to its goal at the bottom.

A walk check then moves a two-voxel-tall explorer through the voxels: walking, stepping up or down one voxel, dropping and climbing shafts. It confirms every cell can be reached and left again. The red line is its route from outside to the goal.

For WFC, each voxel has a label (wall, floor, stair, door, shaft…), and each cell records its region, its kind and a socket on each of its six faces (wall, open, arch, door, entrance, stair, shaft, floor, roof). Markers (◦) are air with a meaning: doorways, shaft space, the entrance, the goal.

One finger drags to orbit, pinch to zoom, two fingers to pan. The bar on the right of the diorama slices the structure: drag it down to hide everything above that height and look inside; the ticks mark each storey. The arrows step through the structures. Settings live in the URL, so a structure can be bookmarked.
